# Nutrition, fuelling and hydration

## Carbohydrate during rides — practical evidence-aware ranges
These are starting ranges, not prescriptions:
- <60 min easy: often no carbohydrate needed during the ride if daily nutrition is adequate.
- 60–120 min moderate/hard: ~30–60 g/h often useful.
- 2–3 h demanding: ~60–90 g/h.
- >3 h / racing: ~80–100+ g/h can be useful for trained athletes; >90 g/h should be practised and individualized, generally using multiple carbohydrate transporters.

Fuel the sessions that need quality. “Train low” approaches should never drift into chronic low energy availability.

## Protein
Use a broad endurance-athlete range rather than a single magic number. A practical default is roughly 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day depending on training load, age, energy availability and goals, distributed across meals; some recent metabolic work supports higher needs under very heavy training or low-carbohydrate availability.

## Hydration
Personalize using:
- sweat rate;
- temperature/humidity;
- ride duration/intensity;
- drink access;
- GI tolerance;
- sodium losses where known.

Avoid both excessive dehydration and overdrinking. Do not prescribe fluid that routinely causes body-mass gain during exercise.

## Caffeine
Optional ergogenic aid. Typical evidence-based range is ~3–6 mg/kg, with some athletes benefiting from less. Higher is not automatically better and can impair sleep or cause adverse effects.

## RED-S safety
Flag persistent low energy intake, rapid weight loss, repeated illness/injury, menstrual disturbance, libido changes, impaired recovery, mood changes or unexplained performance decline for professional assessment rather than giving more restrictive nutrition advice.
