# Training intensity distribution and periodisation

## What is well supported
- A large majority of endurance training is generally low intensity.
- Trained and elite athletes successfully use both pyramidal and polarized distributions.
- Polarized training can provide a small VO2peak advantage in some contexts, but it is not consistently superior for all performance outcomes.
- Training distribution changes by phase, sport and athlete.
- Controlled, repeatable quality sessions are preferable to making every interval session exhaustive.

## App model
Use three physiological domains internally:
1. **Low / moderate domain:** below first threshold (LT1/VT1 equivalent).
2. **Heavy domain:** between first and second threshold / critical-power boundary.
3. **Severe+ domain:** above the second threshold/CP, plus a separate sprint/neuromuscular category.

Display 5–7 familiar cycling zones if desired, but the engine should understand the underlying domains.

## Periodisation approach
Avoid a mandatory 3:1 load/recovery calendar for everyone. A useful default is 2–4 progressive weeks followed by a reduced-load week, but readiness, age, training history and life stress should be allowed to bring recovery earlier.

### Base/foundation
High low-intensity volume, technique/cadence variety, progressive long ride, 1 modest quality session, 2 strength sessions.

### Build
Maintain low-intensity foundation, 1–2 targeted intensity sessions, event-specific work, 1–2 strength sessions.

### Specific/competition
Increase specificity, preserve high-end stimulus, reduce non-specific fatigue, strength maintenance ~1/week.

### Taper
For a priority event, reduce total volume roughly 40–60% over 7–14 days as a common default while retaining frequency and brief intensity. Extend toward 21 days when prior load is unusually high or the athlete historically responds better to a longer taper.
