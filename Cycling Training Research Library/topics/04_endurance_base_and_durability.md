# Endurance base and durability

## Low-intensity work
Low intensity enables high training volume with manageable autonomic, metabolic and musculoskeletal cost. Its value is not that it is magical “fat burning”; it is that it supports repeated aerobic stimulus while preserving capacity for quality work.

## Long ride progression
Progress the long ride gradually and only when the athlete is recovering. For most non-elite riders, consistency across weeks matters more than heroic single-day volume.

## Durability
Durability asks: how much of the rider’s fresh performance remains after prolonged work?

Useful markers:
- % drop in 5-min and 20-min power after standardized prior work;
- HR drift/decoupling at a stable endurance power;
- late-ride RPE inflation;
- ability to execute threshold/tempo after 2–4 h;
- late-event sprint/power preservation.

Train durability through progressive long rides, race-specific late-ride work, adequate fuelling and repeated exposure—not by routinely riding depleted or exhausted.
