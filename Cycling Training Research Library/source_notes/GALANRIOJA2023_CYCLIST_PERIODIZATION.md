# Training Periodization, Intensity Distribution, and Volume in Trained Cyclists: A Systematic Review

**Citation:** Galán-Rioja MA; Gonzalez-Ravé JM; González-Mohíno F; Seiler S (2023). *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance*.  
**Type:** Systematic review  
**Population:** Trained cyclists  
**Directness:** Cycling-specific  
**Evidence grade:** A  
**Open access:** Yes  
**DOI / source:** https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2022-0302

## What it adds
Cycling studies use multiple periodization and intensity-distribution models; the literature does not establish one universally superior model across contexts.

## App implication
Make periodization configurable. Bias toward high low-intensity volume with targeted quality rather than claiming a single optimal distribution.

## Tags
periodization, cycling, TID, volume

## Interpretation caution
This is a concise evidence note, not a reproduction of the paper. Apply findings according to athlete calibre, intervention duration, sex representation, event demands and study limitations. Where the source is not cycling-specific, directness is explicitly downgraded.
