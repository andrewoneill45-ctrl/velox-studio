# Recovery, sleep and monitoring

## Recovery hierarchy
1. sufficient sleep opportunity;
2. adequate energy/carbohydrate/protein and fluid;
3. sensible training-load design;
4. low-intensity/rest days;
5. optional modalities (massage, compression, cold water, etc.).

The recovery-modality literature does not show one universally superior method.

## Daily monitoring
Useful low-friction inputs:
- sleep duration/opportunity and perceived quality;
- subjective fatigue, soreness, mood/motivation;
- resting HR trend;
- HRV trend if measured consistently;
- recent training load and session-RPE;
- completion quality of recent key sessions.

## Readiness algorithm principle
Use a rolling individualized baseline and require multiple agreeing signals before making a major change. A single low HRV or poor night of sleep should not automatically cancel training.

### Example downgrade triggers
- 2+ days of markedly worse subjective fatigue + poor sleep + elevated resting HR;
- inability to hit normal endurance power at unusually high RPE;
- persistent soreness affecting movement;
- illness symptoms.

When illness or medical red flags are present, use a safety pathway rather than a performance algorithm.
