# Evidence grading and implementation rules

## Grade A
Systematic review/meta-analysis, consensus statement, position stand or similarly high-level evidence. A-grade evidence can still be indirect, heterogeneous or low-certainty; check `directness` and the source note.

## Grade B
Controlled intervention, high-quality narrative review, applied best-practice evidence, or older foundational synthesis. Use as a strong input, but avoid converting one protocol into a universal rule.

## Grade C
Evidence that is useful but indirect/limited (for example, yoga evidence from non-cycling athletes). Present as optional rather than essential.

## Rule confidence
For app rules use:
- **High confidence:** multiple A/B sources, directly relevant, low contradiction.
- **Moderate confidence:** good evidence but context/individual response matters.
- **Low confidence:** indirect evidence or coaching convention.

## Evidence freshness
Store `last_reviewed` on every algorithmic rule. Re-run literature review at least annually, and sooner for fast-moving topics such as durability, female-athlete physiology, wearable readiness algorithms and advanced carbohydrate strategies.
