# Progression and load rules

## Progress only when
- planned sessions are being completed without repeated failure;
- endurance RPE/HR response is stable or improving;
- sleep/fatigue are acceptable;
- no emerging pain/illness signal;
- weekly load is compatible with the athlete’s historical tolerance.

## Progression levers (change 1–2, not all at once)
- add easy duration;
- extend long ride;
- add one interval repetition;
- extend work-interval duration;
- reduce recovery slightly;
- raise target power modestly after evidence of adaptation;
- add strength load/sets;
- add event-specific late-ride work.

## Deload triggers
- repeated inability to complete quality sessions;
- rising RPE at ordinary endurance power across several sessions;
- persistent sleep/fatigue/mood deterioration;
- accumulating soreness/pain;
- unusually high life stress.

## Load metrics
TSS/CTL-style metrics can be shown, but should be treated as descriptive estimates. They compress intensity, duration and physiology into one score and should never be the sole progression constraint.
