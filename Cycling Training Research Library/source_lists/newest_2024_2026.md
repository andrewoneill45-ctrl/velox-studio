# Newest high-value sources: 2024–2026



1. **Time spent at or near V̇O₂max during high-intensity interval training - a systematic review and meta-analysis** (2026) — Long intervals of at least ~2 minutes generally generate more accumulated time near VO2max than short/moderate intervals; variable-intensity work can increase time near VO2max.  
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01766-x

2. **Number of Sprint Repetitions in a Sprint Interval Training Session Does Not Moderate Improvements in V̇O₂max With Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** (2026) — Sprint interval training improved VO2max substantially across studies, but doing more sprint repetitions per session did not explain larger improvements.  
   https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.70347

3. **Durability as an index of endurance exercise performance: Methodological considerations** (2025) — Durability is the resilience of physiological/performance variables to deterioration during or after prolonged exercise. Results are strongly influenced by the fatiguing protocol, nutrition and environment.  
   https://doi.org/10.1113/EP092120

4. **Durability as an independent parameter of endurance performance in cycling** (2025) — After 1000 kJ of prior work, 5- and 20-min power fell by about 10% on average; durability was not clearly related to VO2max or relative FTP.  
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-025-01238-8

5. **Consensus Statements—Optimizing Performance of the Elite Athlete (female-athlete section)** (2025) — Current evidence is insufficient to prescribe training, nutrition or injury-prevention changes by menstrual phase; athlete-specific symptoms and clinically valid information should guide adjustments.  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12334928/

6. **Meta-analyses of the effects of high-intensity interval training in elite athletes—part I: mean effects on various performance measures** (2025) — HIIT can improve multiple performance measures even in highly trained athletes; more aerobic long-interval formats appear especially useful for VO2max.  
   https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2024.1486526

7. **Protein Nutrition for Endurance Athletes: A Metabolic Focus on Promoting Recovery and Training Adaptation** (2025) — Endurance athletes may benefit from protein intakes toward the upper end of traditional sports recommendations, especially under heavy load, recovery or low-carbohydrate availability.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02203-8

8. **Heavy strength training effects on physiological determinants of endurance cyclist performance: a systematic review with meta-analysis** (2025) — Heavy strength training (≥80% 1RM) improved cycling efficiency, anaerobic power and cycling performance, without clear effects on VO2max. Benefits appear after sufficient duration/dose.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-025-05883-2

9. **Effect of yoga on selected performance metrics and mental health of track and field athletes: A systematic review** (2025) — Yoga appears useful for flexibility, balance and some wellbeing/performance measures, but direct cycling-performance evidence remains sparse and study quality is limited.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2025.08.006

10. **Training Session Models in Endurance Sports: A Norwegian Perspective on Best Practice Recommendations** (2024) — Successful endurance programmes feature high volumes of low intensity work and controlled, repeatable interval sessions that are often less exhaustive than research intervention protocols.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02067-4

11. **Cardiorespiratory and metabolic consequences of detraining in endurance athletes** (2024) — Training cessation reduces cardiorespiratory and metabolic adaptations; partial training can attenuate losses, while longer cessation causes broader decline.  
   https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1334766

12. **Application of mobility training methods in sporting populations: A systematic review of performance adaptations** (2024) — Mobility interventions can improve range of motion and selected physical-performance measures, but methods and outcomes vary.  
   https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321006

13. **Effectiveness of Recovery Strategies After Training and Competition in Endurance Athletes: An Umbrella Review** (2024) — No recovery modality is consistently superior across outcomes. Some modalities may help particular soreness/perception outcomes, but effects on subsequent performance are inconsistent.  
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-024-00724-6

14. **Comparison of Polarized Versus Other Types of Endurance Training Intensity Distribution on Athletes’ Endurance Performance: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis** (2024) — Polarized training showed a small advantage for VO2peak, particularly in highly trained athletes and interventions under 12 weeks, but was not superior for time trial, time-to-exhaustion, or power/speed at VT2/LT2.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z
