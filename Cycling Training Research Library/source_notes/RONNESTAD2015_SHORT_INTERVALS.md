# Short intervals induce superior training adaptations compared with long intervals in cyclists – an effort-matched approach

**Citation:** Rønnestad BR; Hansen J; Vegge G; Tønnessen E; Slettaløkken G (2015). *Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports*.  
**Type:** Cycling training intervention  
**Population:** Well-trained cyclists  
**Directness:** Cycling-specific  
**Evidence grade:** B  
**Open access:** No / check publisher access  
**DOI / source:** https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12165

## What it adds
In this study, short work/rest intervals produced strong adaptations compared with longer interval sessions under the tested design.

## App implication
Keep 30/15-style microintervals as an alternative VO2 format, especially for riders who struggle to sustain long intervals.

## Tags
cycling, 30/15, HIIT

## Interpretation caution
This is a concise evidence note, not a reproduction of the paper. Apply findings according to athlete calibre, intervention duration, sex representation, event demands and study limitations. Where the source is not cycling-specific, directness is explicitly downgraded.
