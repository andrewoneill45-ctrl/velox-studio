# Open-access sources



1. **Comparison of Polarized Versus Other Types of Endurance Training Intensity Distribution on Athletes’ Endurance Performance: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis** (2024) — Polarized training showed a small advantage for VO2peak, particularly in highly trained athletes and interventions under 12 weeks, but was not superior for time trial, time-to-exhaustion, or power/speed at VT2/LT2.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z

2. **Training Periodization, Intensity Distribution, and Volume in Trained Cyclists: A Systematic Review** (2023) — Cycling studies use multiple periodization and intensity-distribution models; the literature does not establish one universally superior model across contexts.  
   https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2022-0302

3. **The proportional distribution of training by elite endurance athletes at different intensities during different phases of the season** (2023) — Across reported elite-athlete training distributions, pyramidal and polarized patterns were both common and varied by sport and phase of season.  
   https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2023.1258585

4. **Training Session Models in Endurance Sports: A Norwegian Perspective on Best Practice Recommendations** (2024) — Successful endurance programmes feature high volumes of low intensity work and controlled, repeatable interval sessions that are often less exhaustive than research intervention protocols.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02067-4

5. **Polarized training has greater impact on key endurance variables than threshold, high intensity, or high volume training** (2014) — In this intervention, a polarized programme produced strong improvements in several endurance outcomes compared with alternative distributions.  
   https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2014.00033

6. **Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysis** (2023) — Tapering improves time-trial/time-to-exhaustion performance. The best-supported approach reduces volume about 41–60%, keeps intensity and frequency, and lasts up to 21 days.  
   https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0282838

7. **Time spent at or near V̇O₂max during high-intensity interval training - a systematic review and meta-analysis** (2026) — Long intervals of at least ~2 minutes generally generate more accumulated time near VO2max than short/moderate intervals; variable-intensity work can increase time near VO2max.  
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01766-x

8. **Meta-analyses of the effects of high-intensity interval training in elite athletes—part I: mean effects on various performance measures** (2025) — HIIT can improve multiple performance measures even in highly trained athletes; more aerobic long-interval formats appear especially useful for VO2max.  
   https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2024.1486526

9. **Number of Sprint Repetitions in a Sprint Interval Training Session Does Not Moderate Improvements in V̇O₂max With Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** (2026) — Sprint interval training improved VO2max substantially across studies, but doing more sprint repetitions per session did not explain larger improvements.  
   https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.70347

10. **Power profiling and the power-duration relationship in cycling: a narrative review** (2022) — Structured multi-duration testing and power-duration modelling provide a richer cyclist fingerprint than single-effort metrics; the authors recommend avoiding single-effort prediction trials such as FTP as the sole model.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-021-04833-y

11. **Durability as an index of endurance exercise performance: Methodological considerations** (2025) — Durability is the resilience of physiological/performance variables to deterioration during or after prolonged exercise. Results are strongly influenced by the fatiguing protocol, nutrition and environment.  
   https://doi.org/10.1113/EP092120

12. **Durability as an independent parameter of endurance performance in cycling** (2025) — After 1000 kJ of prior work, 5- and 20-min power fell by about 10% on average; durability was not clearly related to VO2max or relative FTP.  
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-025-01238-8

13. **Heavy strength training effects on physiological determinants of endurance cyclist performance: a systematic review with meta-analysis** (2025) — Heavy strength training (≥80% 1RM) improved cycling efficiency, anaerobic power and cycling performance, without clear effects on VO2max. Benefits appear after sufficient duration/dose.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-025-05883-2

14. **Compatibility of Concurrent Aerobic and Strength Training for Skeletal Muscle Size and Function: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** (2022) — Concurrent endurance + strength generally does not compromise hypertrophy or maximal strength, but explosive strength gains can be attenuated, especially when both are performed in the same session.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7

15. **Chronic Effects of Static Stretching Exercises on Muscle Strength and Power in Healthy Individuals Across the Lifespan: A Systematic Review with Multi-level Meta-analysis** (2023) — Long-term static stretching improves flexibility and may produce trivial-to-small positive strength/power effects; it is not inherently harmful when programmed appropriately.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01806-9

16. **Application of mobility training methods in sporting populations: A systematic review of performance adaptations** (2024) — Mobility interventions can improve range of motion and selected physical-performance measures, but methods and outcomes vary.  
   https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321006

17. **Effectiveness of Recovery Strategies After Training and Competition in Endurance Athletes: An Umbrella Review** (2024) — No recovery modality is consistently superior across outcomes. Some modalities may help particular soreness/perception outcomes, but effects on subsequent performance are inconsistent.  
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-024-00724-6

18. **Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement** (2017) — Training-load monitoring should combine internal and external load measures and be interpreted in context; there is no single best metric.  
   https://doi.org/10.1123/IJSPP.2017-0208

19. **Heart Rate Variability-Guided Training for Enhancing Cardiac-Vagal Modulation, Aerobic Fitness, and Endurance Performance: A Methodological Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis** (2021) — HRV-guided training can improve some aerobic/physiological outcomes and may reduce non-response, but performance advantages are modest/inconsistent and protocols differ.  
   https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph181910299

20. **New Horizons in Carbohydrate Research and Application for Endurance Athletes** (2022) — Carbohydrate remains central to prolonged/high-intensity endurance performance. Multiple-transportable carbohydrate strategies enable high exogenous oxidation; intakes above traditional 90 g/h are an emerging advanced option for some athletes/events.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01757-1

21. **Protein Nutrition for Endurance Athletes: A Metabolic Focus on Promoting Recovery and Training Adaptation** (2025) — Endurance athletes may benefit from protein intakes toward the upper end of traditional sports recommendations, especially under heavy load, recovery or low-carbohydrate availability.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02203-8

22. **2023 International Olympic Committee’s consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)** (2023) — Problematic low energy availability can impair health and performance in athletes of all sexes; low carbohydrate availability can contribute to risk.  
   https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994

23. **International society of sports nutrition position stand: caffeine and exercise performance** (2021) — Caffeine can improve endurance and other performance outcomes for many athletes; common effective doses are around 3–6 mg/kg, with some benefit at lower doses and greater side-effect risk at high doses.  
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4

24. **Consensus recommendations on training and competing in the heat** (2015) — Heat acclimation is an effective preparation strategy; repeated heat exposures over roughly 1–2 weeks produce substantial cardiovascular and thermoregulatory adaptation.  
   https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-094915

25. **Cardiorespiratory and metabolic consequences of detraining in endurance athletes** (2024) — Training cessation reduces cardiorespiratory and metabolic adaptations; partial training can attenuate losses, while longer cessation causes broader decline.  
   https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1334766

26. **Defining Training and Performance Caliber: A Participant Classification Framework** (2022) — A six-tier framework distinguishes sedentary through world-class participants, addressing inconsistent use of labels such as “elite”.  
   https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2021-0451

27. **The Effects of Menstrual Cycle Phase on Exercise Performance in Eumenorrheic Women: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** (2020) — Average performance differences across menstrual-cycle phases were trivial, with large between-study variation and low-quality evidence limiting phase-based prescriptions.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-020-01319-3

28. **Consensus Statements—Optimizing Performance of the Elite Athlete (female-athlete section)** (2025) — Current evidence is insufficient to prescribe training, nutrition or injury-prevention changes by menstrual phase; athlete-specific symptoms and clinically valid information should guide adjustments.  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12334928/

29. **Endurance exercise performance: the physiology of champions** (2008) — Endurance performance can be understood through interacting determinants including VO2max, sustainable fraction/threshold and economy/efficiency, shaped by training and morphology.  
   https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2007.143834
