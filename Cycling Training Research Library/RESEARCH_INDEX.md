# Research index

Curated sources: **47**. Evidence review date: **2026-08-31**.

## Applied endurance programming
- **[B] 2024 — Training Session Models in Endurance Sports: A Norwegian Perspective on Best Practice Recommendations** — Successful endurance programmes feature high volumes of low intensity work and controlled, repeatable interval sessions that are often less exhaustive than research intervention protocols. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02067-4))

## Detraining and return
- **[A] 2024 — Cardiorespiratory and metabolic consequences of detraining in endurance athletes** — Training cessation reduces cardiorespiratory and metabolic adaptations; partial training can attenuate losses, while longer cessation causes broader decline. ([source](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1334766))

## Durability
- **[B] 2025 — Durability as an index of endurance exercise performance: Methodological considerations** — Durability is the resilience of physiological/performance variables to deterioration during or after prolonged exercise. Results are strongly influenced by the fatiguing protocol, nutrition and environment. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1113/EP092120))
- **[B] 2025 — Durability as an independent parameter of endurance performance in cycling** — After 1000 kJ of prior work, 5- and 20-min power fell by about 10% on average; durability was not clearly related to VO2max or relative FTP. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-025-01238-8))

## Environment
- **[A] 2015 — Consensus recommendations on training and competing in the heat** — Heat acclimation is an effective preparation strategy; repeated heat exposures over roughly 1–2 weeks produce substantial cardiovascular and thermoregulatory adaptation. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-094915))

## Female athlete considerations
- **[A] 2025 — Consensus Statements—Optimizing Performance of the Elite Athlete (female-athlete section)** — Current evidence is insufficient to prescribe training, nutrition or injury-prevention changes by menstrual phase; athlete-specific symptoms and clinically valid information should guide adjustments. ([source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12334928/))
- **[A] 2020 — The Effects of Menstrual Cycle Phase on Exercise Performance in Eumenorrheic Women: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** — Average performance differences across menstrual-cycle phases were trivial, with large between-study variation and low-quality evidence limiting phase-based prescriptions. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-020-01319-3))

## Foundations
- **[B] 2008 — Endurance exercise performance: the physiology of champions** — Endurance performance can be understood through interacting determinants including VO2max, sustainable fraction/threshold and economy/efficiency, shaped by training and morphology. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2007.143834))

## HIIT and VO2max
- **[A] 2026 — Time spent at or near V̇O₂max during high-intensity interval training - a systematic review and meta-analysis** — Long intervals of at least ~2 minutes generally generate more accumulated time near VO2max than short/moderate intervals; variable-intensity work can increase time near VO2max. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01766-x))
- **[A] 2026 — Number of Sprint Repetitions in a Sprint Interval Training Session Does Not Moderate Improvements in V̇O₂max With Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** — Sprint interval training improved VO2max substantially across studies, but doing more sprint repetitions per session did not explain larger improvements. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.70347))
- **[A] 2025 — Meta-analyses of the effects of high-intensity interval training in elite athletes—part I: mean effects on various performance measures** — HIIT can improve multiple performance measures even in highly trained athletes; more aerobic long-interval formats appear especially useful for VO2max. ([source](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2024.1486526))
- **[B] 2015 — Short intervals induce superior training adaptations compared with long intervals in cyclists – an effort-matched approach** — In this study, short work/rest intervals produced strong adaptations compared with longer interval sessions under the tested design. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12165))
- **[B] 2013 — High-Intensity Interval Training, Solutions to the Programming Puzzle: Part I: Cardiopulmonary Emphasis** — HIIT responses are determined by multiple variables including work duration, intensity, recovery duration, recovery intensity and modality. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0029-x))
- **[B] 2013 — High-Intensity Interval Training, Solutions to the Programming Puzzle: Part II: Anaerobic Energy, Neuromuscular Load and Practical Applications** — Different HIIT formats alter anaerobic contribution, neuromuscular load and recovery cost. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0066-5))
- **[B] 2002 — The Scientific Basis for High-Intensity Interval Training: Optimising Training Programmes and Maximising Performance in Highly Trained Endurance Athletes** — HIIT is a potent tool for trained endurance athletes but must be carefully dosed and integrated with lower-intensity work. ([source](https://doi.org/10.2165/00007256-200232010-00003))
- **[B] 1999 — Effects of different interval-training programs on cycling time-trial performance** — Different high-intensity interval formats can improve time-trial performance, reinforcing the importance of matching interval design to the athlete and target. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005768-199905000-00018))

## Hydration
- **[A] 2017 — The Influence of Drinking Fluid on Endurance Cycling Performance: A Meta-Analysis** — The effect of drinking during cycling is duration- and intensity-dependent; fluid helped longer moderate-intensity cycling in the included studies but could impose a performance cost in shorter high-intensity trials. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0739-6))
- **[A] 2007 — American College of Sports Medicine position stand. Exercise and fluid replacement** — Hydration strategies should aim to avoid excessive dehydration and overdrinking, with individual sweat rates and environmental demands considered. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31802ca597))

## Injury and bike fit
- **[A] 2022 — Factors associated with overuse injury in cyclists: A systematic review** — Evidence linking many specific bike/body-fit measures to overuse injury is weak; previous symptoms and load-related factors have more consistent support than simplistic fit rules. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2021.12.008))

## Mobility and stretching
- **[B] 2024 — Application of mobility training methods in sporting populations: A systematic review of performance adaptations** — Mobility interventions can improve range of motion and selected physical-performance measures, but methods and outcomes vary. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321006))
- **[A] 2023 — Chronic Effects of Static Stretching Exercises on Muscle Strength and Power in Healthy Individuals Across the Lifespan: A Systematic Review with Multi-level Meta-analysis** — Long-term static stretching improves flexibility and may produce trivial-to-small positive strength/power effects; it is not inherently harmful when programmed appropriately. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01806-9))

## Monitoring
- **[B] 2021 — Heart Rate Variability-Guided Training for Enhancing Cardiac-Vagal Modulation, Aerobic Fitness, and Endurance Performance: A Methodological Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis** — HRV-guided training can improve some aerobic/physiological outcomes and may reduce non-response, but performance advantages are modest/inconsistent and protocols differ. ([source](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph181910299))
- **[A] 2017 — Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement** — Training-load monitoring should combine internal and external load measures and be interpreted in context; there is no single best metric. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1123/IJSPP.2017-0208))
- **[B] 2001 — A new approach to monitoring exercise training** — Session-RPE provides a simple, low-cost measure of internal training load that can complement objective workload. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1519/00124278-200102000-00019))

## Nutrition
- **[B] 2025 — Protein Nutrition for Endurance Athletes: A Metabolic Focus on Promoting Recovery and Training Adaptation** — Endurance athletes may benefit from protein intakes toward the upper end of traditional sports recommendations, especially under heavy load, recovery or low-carbohydrate availability. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02203-8))
- **[B] 2022 — New Horizons in Carbohydrate Research and Application for Endurance Athletes** — Carbohydrate remains central to prolonged/high-intensity endurance performance. Multiple-transportable carbohydrate strategies enable high exogenous oxidation; intakes above traditional 90 g/h are an emerging advanced option for some athletes/events. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01757-1))
- **[A] 2016 — Nutrition and Athletic Performance** — Energy, carbohydrate, protein, fluid and micronutrient intake should be periodized to support training demands and recovery. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006))

## Nutrition and ergogenic aids
- **[A] 2021 — International society of sports nutrition position stand: caffeine and exercise performance** — Caffeine can improve endurance and other performance outcomes for many athletes; common effective doses are around 3–6 mg/kg, with some benefit at lower doses and greater side-effect risk at high doses. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4))

## Nutrition and health
- **[A] 2023 — 2023 International Olympic Committee’s consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)** — Problematic low energy availability can impair health and performance in athletes of all sexes; low carbohydrate availability can contribute to risk. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994))

## Periodization and taper
- **[A] 2023 — Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysis** — Tapering improves time-trial/time-to-exhaustion performance. The best-supported approach reduces volume about 41–60%, keeps intensity and frequency, and lasts up to 21 days. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0282838))
- **[A] 2007 — Effects of tapering on performance: a meta-analysis** — Classic taper meta-analysis supports approximately two weeks of tapering with a substantial progressive reduction in volume while intensity is maintained. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0))

## Recovery
- **[A] 2024 — Effectiveness of Recovery Strategies After Training and Competition in Endurance Athletes: An Umbrella Review** — No recovery modality is consistently superior across outcomes. Some modalities may help particular soreness/perception outcomes, but effects on subsequent performance are inconsistent. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-024-00724-6))
- **[A] 2021 — Sleep and the athlete: narrative review and 2021 expert consensus recommendations** — Athletes have individual sleep needs and are vulnerable to sleep restriction. Sleep extension, naps and sleep-hygiene strategies can be useful when individualized. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102025))
- **[A] 2013 — Prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of the overtraining syndrome: joint consensus statement of the ECSS and ACSM** — Functional overreaching, non-functional overreaching and overtraining syndrome are clinically distinct concepts; persistent performance decline requires exclusion of illness and other causes. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e318279a10a))

## Research quality
- **[A] 2022 — Defining Training and Performance Caliber: A Participant Classification Framework** — A six-tier framework distinguishes sedentary through world-class participants, addressing inconsistent use of labels such as “elite”. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2021-0451))

## Strength training
- **[A] 2025 — Heavy strength training effects on physiological determinants of endurance cyclist performance: a systematic review with meta-analysis** — Heavy strength training (≥80% 1RM) improved cycling efficiency, anaerobic power and cycling performance, without clear effects on VO2max. Benefits appear after sufficient duration/dose. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-025-05883-2))
- **[A] 2022 — Compatibility of Concurrent Aerobic and Strength Training for Skeletal Muscle Size and Function: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis** — Concurrent endurance + strength generally does not compromise hypertrophy or maximal strength, but explosive strength gains can be attenuated, especially when both are performed in the same session. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7))
- **[B] 2015 — Strength training improves performance and pedaling characteristics in elite cyclists** — Long-term heavy strength training improved several cycling performance determinants and performance outcomes in well-trained cyclists. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12257))
- **[B] 2014 — Optimizing strength training for running and cycling endurance performance: A review** — Heavy strength training can improve endurance performance determinants through neuromuscular and efficiency-related mechanisms, with appropriate integration into endurance training. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12104))

## Testing and physiology
- **[B] 2022 — Power profiling and the power-duration relationship in cycling: a narrative review** — Structured multi-duration testing and power-duration modelling provide a richer cyclist fingerprint than single-effort metrics; the authors recommend avoiding single-effort prediction trials such as FTP as the sole model. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-021-04833-y))
- **[B] 2021 — What is known about the FTP20 test related to cycling? A scoping review** — FTP20 is generally reliable but shows limits of agreement with physiological threshold markers large enough that the measures should not be treated as interchangeable. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2021.1955515))

## Training intensity distribution
- **[A] 2024 — Comparison of Polarized Versus Other Types of Endurance Training Intensity Distribution on Athletes’ Endurance Performance: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis** — Polarized training showed a small advantage for VO2peak, particularly in highly trained athletes and interventions under 12 weeks, but was not superior for time trial, time-to-exhaustion, or power/speed at VT2/LT2. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z))
- **[A] 2023 — Training Periodization, Intensity Distribution, and Volume in Trained Cyclists: A Systematic Review** — Cycling studies use multiple periodization and intensity-distribution models; the literature does not establish one universally superior model across contexts. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2022-0302))
- **[B] 2023 — The proportional distribution of training by elite endurance athletes at different intensities during different phases of the season** — Across reported elite-athlete training distributions, pyramidal and polarized patterns were both common and varied by sport and phase of season. ([source](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2023.1258585))
- **[B] 2014 — Polarized training has greater impact on key endurance variables than threshold, high intensity, or high volume training** — In this intervention, a polarized programme produced strong improvements in several endurance outcomes compared with alternative distributions. ([source](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2014.00033))
- **[B] 2010 — What is Best Practice for Training Intensity and Duration Distribution in Endurance Athletes?** — A large proportion of successful endurance training is performed at low intensity, with a smaller but important dose of high intensity work. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.5.3.276))

## Yoga
- **[C] 2025 — Effect of yoga on selected performance metrics and mental health of track and field athletes: A systematic review** — Yoga appears useful for flexibility, balance and some wellbeing/performance measures, but direct cycling-performance evidence remains sparse and study quality is limited. ([source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbmt.2025.08.006))
