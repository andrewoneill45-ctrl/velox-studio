# Strength training for cyclists

## Evidence-based priority
Heavy lower-body strength has direct cycling evidence for improving cycling performance, efficiency and anaerobic power even when VO2max does not change.

## Development phase (8–16+ weeks)
2 sessions/week when healthy and schedule allows.

Core exercise menu (select 3–4):
- squat or leg press;
- Romanian deadlift / trap-bar deadlift / hip hinge;
- split squat or step-up;
- calf raise;
- optional hamstring curl;
- small dose trunk anti-extension/anti-rotation work.

### Loading
After a technical adaptation phase, bias toward ~3–6 repetitions with heavy loads (~80–90% 1RM), 2–4 work sets, long rests and 1–3 reps in reserve. Do not make failure training the default.

## Maintenance phase
~1 heavy session/week can preserve much of the strength adaptation while reducing interference with cycling-specific work.

## Scheduling
Best options:
- strength on a separate day from key intervals; or
- same day as intensity to consolidate stress, separated by several hours, depending on athlete tolerance.

Avoid heavy lower-body lifting immediately before the most important bike quality session. Same-session concurrent work is more likely to compromise explosive-strength development than separated sessions.

## “Core”
Trunk strength can support comfort/control, but evidence for core-only programmes improving cycling performance is much weaker than evidence for heavy lower-body strength. Keep it brief and purposeful.
