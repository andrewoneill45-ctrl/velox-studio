2017HydrationThe Influence of Drinking Fluid on Endurance Cycling Performance: A Meta-AnalysisAThe effect of drinking during cycling is duration- and intensity-dependent; fluid helped longer moderate-intensity cycling in the included studies but could impose a performance cost in shorter high-intensity trials.Individualize fluid intake by duration, intensity, heat and tolerance rather than mandating maximal replacement. Avoid both excessive dehydration and overdrinking.

Exercise and fluid replacementAHydration strategies should aim to avoid excessive dehydration and overdrinking, with individual sweat rates and environmental demands considered.Estimate sweat rate from pre/post body mass when practical. 2024Mobility and stretchingApplication of mobility training methods in sporting populations: A systematic review of performance adaptationsBOAMobility interventions can improve range of motion and selected physical-performance measures, but methods and outcomes vary.Offer short mobility routines based on measured restriction or rider comfort, not mandatory generic mobility volume for everyone.
2023Mobility and stretchingChronic Effects of Static Stretching Exercises on Muscle Strength and Power in Healthy Individuals Across the Lifespan: A Systematic Review with Multi-level Meta-analysisAOALong-term static stretching improves flexibility and may produce trivial-to-small positive strength/power effects; it is not inherently harmful when programmed appropriately.Use stretching mainly for range of motion and comfort goals. Do not market it as a major cycling-performance intervention.
2021MonitoringHeart Rate Variability-Guided Training for Enhancing Cardiac-Vagal Modulation, Aerobic Fitness, and Endurance Performance: A Methodological Systematic Review with Meta-AnalysisBOAHRV-guided training can improve some aerobic/physiological outcomes and may reduce non-response, but performance advantages are modest/inconsistent and protocols differ.Use HRV as one readiness input with individualized baselines and trends. 2017MonitoringMonitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus StatementAOATraining-load monitoring should combine internal and external load measures and be interpreted in context; there is no single best metric.Combine power/duration (external load), session RPE/HR (internal load), and subjective state. 2001MonitoringA new approach to monitoring exercise trainingBSession-RPE provides a simple, low-cost measure of internal training load that can complement objective workload.Collect session RPE after every ride/strength session. 2025NutritionProtein Nutrition for Endurance Athletes: A Metabolic Focus on Promoting Recovery and Training AdaptationBOAEndurance athletes may benefit from protein intakes toward the upper end of traditional sports recommendations, especially under heavy load, recovery or low-carbohydrate availability.Use a broad evidence-aware protein range rather than a rigid value; distribute protein across meals and prioritize sufficient total energy/carbohydrate first.
2022NutritionNew Horizons in Carbohydrate Research and Application for Endurance AthletesBOACarbohydrate remains central to prolonged/high-intensity endurance performance. 2016NutritionNutrition and Athletic PerformanceAEnergy, carbohydrate, protein, fluid and micronutrient intake should be periodized to support training demands and recovery.Tie fuelling prompts to session duration/intensity and daily training load rather than giving one fixed diet prescription.
2021Nutrition and ergogenic aidsInternational society of sports nutrition position stand: caffeine and exercise performanceAOACaffeine can improve endurance and other performance outcomes for many athletes; common effective doses are around 3–6 mg/kg, with some benefit at lower doses and greater side-effect risk at high doses.If included, make caffeine optional, individualized and bounded by sleep, tolerance, medical considerations and competition rules. Do not normalize high dosing.
2023Nutrition and health2023 International Olympic Committee’s consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)AOAProblematic low energy availability can impair health and performance in athletes of all sexes; low carbohydrate availability can contribute to risk.Never encourage chronic under-fuelling for weight loss or “train low” without safeguards. Add RED-S warning flags and refer persistent symptoms to sports medicine/dietetic professionals.
2023Periodization and taperEffects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysisAOATapering improves time-trial/time-to-exhaustion performance. The best-supported approach reduces volume about 41–60%, keeps intensity and frequency, and lasts up to 21 days.For priority events, create 7–14 day default tapers (extend toward 21 days for large accumulated load) with ~40–60% volume reduction while retaining short intensity exposures.
2007Periodization and taperEffects of tapering on performance: a meta-analysisAClassic taper meta-analysis supports approximately two weeks of tapering with a substantial progressive reduction in volume while intensity is maintained.Use as foundational taper logic and cross-check with newer Wang 2023 synthesis.
2024RecoveryEffectiveness of Recovery Strategies After Training and Competition in Endurance Athletes: An Umbrella ReviewAOANo recovery modality is consistently superior across outcomes. Some modalities may help particular soreness/perception outcomes, but effects on subsequent performance are inconsistent.Prioritize sleep, nutrition, rest and load management. Treat massage, compression, cold water and similar tools as optional, context-dependent add-ons.
2021RecoverySleep and the athlete: narrative review and 2021 expert consensus recommendationsAAthletes have individual sleep needs and are vulnerable to sleep restriction. Sleep extension, naps and sleep-hygiene strategies can be useful when individualized.Track sleep opportunity and perceived sleep quality, not just wearable stages. 2022Research qualityDefining Training and Performance Caliber: A Participant Classification FrameworkAOAA six-tier framework distinguishes sedentary through world-class participants, addressing inconsistent use of labels such as “elite”.Store an athlete-calibre tag on every evidence rule so findings from untrained participants are not automatically applied to national/elite riders.
2025Strength trainingHeavy strength training effects on physiological determinants of endurance cyclist performance: a systematic review with meta-analysisAOAHeavy strength training (≥80% 1RM) improved cycling efficiency, anaerobic power and cycling performance, without clear effects on VO2max. 2022Strength trainingCompatibility of Concurrent Aerobic and Strength Training for Skeletal Muscle Size and Function: An Updated Systematic Review and Meta-AnalysisAOAConcurrent endurance + strength generally does not compromise hypertrophy or maximal strength, but explosive strength gains can be attenuated, especially when both are performed in the same session.Separate key strength and key endurance sessions by several hours or different days when possible. 2014Strength trainingOptimizing strength training for running and cycling endurance performance: A reviewBHeavy strength training can improve endurance performance determinants through neuromuscular and efficiency-related mechanisms, with appropriate integration into endurance training.Prioritize maximal strength and neural quality over bodybuilding-style fatigue. 2023Training intensity distributionTraining Periodization, Intensity Distribution, and Volume in Trained Cyclists: A Systematic ReviewAOACycling studies use multiple periodization and intensity-distribution models; the literature does not establish one universally superior model across contexts.Make periodization configurable.
