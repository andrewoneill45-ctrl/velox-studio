# HIIT, VO2max and interval design

## Principle
HIIT is a family of workouts, not one workout. Manipulating work duration, intensity, recovery duration, recovery intensity and number of repetitions changes the stimulus and fatigue cost.

## Evidence-led templates
### Long aerobic intervals
- 4 × 4–5 min hard, 2–4 min recovery
- 5 × 3–4 min hard, 2–3 min recovery
Goal: accumulate substantial time at high oxygen uptake without sprinting the opening minute.

### Variable / microintervals
- 3 sets of 8–13 × 30 s hard / 15 s float, 3–5 min easy between sets
Useful for some riders who achieve more high-aerobic time with short oscillations.

### Threshold / maximal steady-state development
- 3 × 10–15 min controlled around individualized threshold domain
- 2 × 20 min controlled when appropriate
These are not VO2max sessions; they target sustained high aerobic work and event specificity.

### Sprint interval training
All-out 20–30 s repeats can improve VO2max but are metabolically and psychologically costly. Newer meta-analysis indicates that more repetitions do not necessarily produce greater VO2max gains. Use sparingly and only where the athlete tolerates maximal sprinting safely.

## Quality control
Hard sessions should usually be repeatable. Stop or downgrade if power falls markedly, technique deteriorates, RPE becomes disproportionate, or the session turns into repeated failure.
